The Muslim matrimony space has a trust problem — not because platforms are dishonest, but because the stakes are uniquely high.

When you're looking for a spouse, you're not evaluating a product. You're considering handing the most intimate part of your life to a person you've only ever seen through a screen. That asymmetry of vulnerability is enormous.

Competitors have responded with volume: 4 million verified users. 80 lakh success stories. Those numbers are reassuring. But they answer the wrong question.

The question a young Muslim woman asks is not "How many people used this app?"

She asks: *"Is the person reaching out to me actually who they say they are?"*
Her father asks: *"Can I trust this process with my daughter?"*

At Date Khaas, we built the trust architecture around those two questions — not the headline metrics:

→ Photos blurred by default. Unlocked one person at a time, by the user.
→ ID verification and selfie matching before anyone can initiate contact.
→ Wali accounts — guardians get read access to their family member's conversations.

We are in peak wedding season right now. Families are searching. And the app they trust enough to use is the one that treats verification as a dignity signal, not just a security checkbox.

That's the product we're building.
