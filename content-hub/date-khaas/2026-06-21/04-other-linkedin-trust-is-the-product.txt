In Muslim matrimony, trust isn’t a feature. It’s the entire product.

Here is what the data shows: 77.8% of Muslim singles now find their spouse through a digital platform. That is not a niche trend — that is how our community finds its other half.

But the number one reason families still hesitate? They do not trust the platform.

And they are right to be cautious.

Leading apps are still reporting fake and married profiles slipping through. Photo privacy is treated as a premium add-on, not a default right. Wali involvement is an afterthought, not a design principle.

At Date Khaas, we built it the other way around:

→ Verification happens before your profile is ever shown to anyone
→ Photos are private by default — visible only when you choose
→ The wali can be part of the conversation from message one

This is not about features. It is about fiqh.

The nikah is described as a mithāqan ghalīẓan — a solemn, weighty covenant. If we are serious about honoring that, the platform holding the introductions must be held to the same standard.

If you are building in the Muslim consumer space, the question worth asking is not “what features do users want?”

It is: “are we designing for the transaction, or for the trust?”

Trust is not a moat you build after launch. It is the foundation you pour before the first brick goes up.
