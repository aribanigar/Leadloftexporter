# Competitor product notes - week of 2026-06-29

Study of how the market PRODUCTS present (not how they talk). Never named in any ViaKashmir output.

## What they show
- **MakeMyTrip / Goibibo**: hero is a single large destination photo with a short, benefit-led
  headline overlaid; one filled primary CTA in a warm/contrasting colour. Above the fold is
  one offer, not a grid - the product is the image. Urgency is gentle and factual ("filling
  fast", seats/rooms left), never shouty. Below: 2-3 product cards max per screen, each a photo
  + one line + price chip. Season hooks ("monsoon escapes", "hill stations") drive the lead module.
- **Booking.com**: utilitarian - search bar first, then property cards with a real photo,
  rating, and a short scarcity nudge ("Only 2 left on our site"). Trust signals (free
  cancellation, no prepayment) sit right on the CTA. Partner/"List your property" pitch is a
  separate, reassurance-led funnel: "Reach travellers worldwide", "Set your own prices",
  "No sign-up fee" - loss-aversion plus zero-risk framing.
- **goIndigo**: flat, fast, fare-first; strong single accent colour, lots of whitespace, one
  clear CTA per block. Not a tone we mirror (price-led), but the clean one-action-per-screen
  discipline is worth copying.

## What we should copy this week
- Keep leading with ONE breathtaking photo + a short confident headline; resist adding a second
  competing module above the fold. Our Alpine hero already does this - protect it.
- Put the reassurance/trust line ON or right under the CTA (we do "No cost to list", "Open all
  season"). For B2B, mirror Booking's zero-risk partner framing: "no cost", "your guests", "your
  relationship" - exactly the loss-aversion-plus-safety combo that converts operators.
- Factual gentle urgency only ("the search is happening now", "rooms filling next door"), never
  a countdown or "!".

## One thing to avoid
- The dense multi-card grid above the fold (the OTA homepage wall of deals). It reads as a
  marketplace, not an invitation; it fights our editorial, image-forward feel. One photo, one
  idea, one CTA per screen.
